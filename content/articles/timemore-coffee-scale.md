Picking a scale for espresso shouldn't be complicated, but the market for compact coffee scales has gotten crowded enough that the wrong choice costs you weeks of inconsistent shots. Timemore makes some of the most widely used options in the category, and for good reason , the build quality and response speed are real differentiators at this price band. If you're building out a home espresso or pour-over setup, the [Accessories & Setup](/accessories/) section covers the full context for where a scale fits into the workflow.

The main decision here isn't whether to buy a Timemore , it's which one, and whether one non-Timemore option in this category earns a place in the conversation. Those questions have clear answers.

## What to Look For in a Coffee Scale

### Response Speed and Stabilization Time

A kitchen scale that takes two seconds to register a weight change is fine for measuring flour. For espresso, it's a problem. You're pulling a shot in roughly 25, 30 seconds, and if the scale is chasing a reading for the last five of them, your yield data is meaningless. Response speed , how quickly the scale detects a weight change and stabilizes to a final number , is the single most important technical criterion for espresso scales specifically.

The difference between a 200ms response scale and a 500ms response scale sounds small in isolation. Over a hundred shots it compounds into systematically inaccurate yield data. Timemore has invested in this area, and it shows in how their scales behave compared to basic kitchen scales repurposed for coffee.

### Resolution and Accuracy

Most coffee scales operate at 0.1g resolution. That's appropriate for espresso, where a 0.2g difference in dose can shift extraction meaningfully. Scales rated at 1g resolution are kitchen tools; they're not suitable for espresso work. Pour-over is more forgiving, but 0.1g resolution is still the right standard if you're dialing in consistently.

Accuracy and resolution aren't the same thing. A scale can display 0.1g increments and still drift by 0.5g across the weighing range. Calibration matters. Timemore's mid-range and above models are accurate across the range you'd actually use; the basic models hold up well too, though if you're planning to weigh larger batch quantities you'll want to verify the spec sheet.

### Timer Integration

A dedicated scale timer is functionally different from pulling out your phone. You want the timer to start automatically when water hits the bed, or at minimum to be a single-tap operation while your hands are managing the portafilter. A separate stopwatch introduces one more variable.

Scales with integrated timers eliminate the gymnastics. All five options in this review include timer functionality , that's table stakes for an espresso-focused scale. The question is how the timer is triggered and how clearly it reads under the lighting conditions of your actual kitchen setup.

### Form Factor and Counter Fit

A 53mm portafilter basket and a drip tray have physical dimensions. The scale has to fit under the group head with the cup on it, or sit on the drip tray without fouling the cup's position. Mini form factors exist precisely because standard drip trays on home espresso machines are shallow.

Counter real estate is also genuinely finite. If you're working with a tight setup , and most home espresso setups involve more equipment than counter space reasonably accommodates , a thinner, lighter scale that stores easily matters. Exploring the broader [Accessories & Setup](/accessories/) landscape before committing to any single piece of equipment helps you see how everything fits together before you've already bought the wrong thing.

## Buying Guide

### Espresso vs. Pour-Over: Different Demands

Espresso and pour-over are not equally demanding on a scale. Espresso requires fast response , you're managing a moving weight during a 25-second extraction window, and a slow-stabilizing scale gives you inaccurate yield data. Pour-over is slower-paced; response speed matters less than accuracy over a longer pour. If espresso is your primary use, prioritize response speed. If pour-over is all you're doing, any of these options will serve you well without needing to select specifically for espresso performance.

### Mini vs. Standard Size

The form factor decision depends on your machine's drip tray clearance and your available counter space. Measure the clearance between your drip tray and the bottom of your portafilter basket before buying. If the clearance is tight, a standard-profile scale won't fit with a cup on it. The Mini models in this lineup are specifically designed to fit in constrained spaces. If you have adequate clearance, the standard Basic 2.0 profile offers a slightly larger display and more stable surface.

### Battery vs. Rechargeable

Daily espresso use means the scale is on every morning. Battery-dependent scales require periodic replacement , for daily use, expect to replace batteries roughly every two to four months depending on usage patterns and battery quality. That's a minor friction cost that compounds over time. Rechargeable USB-C models, like the Maestri House option, eliminate that maintenance loop entirely. The tradeoff is that if the internal battery degrades over years of use, the replacement path is less straightforward than swapping AAA cells. Neither option is objectively superior; it depends on which maintenance pattern you find less annoying.

### Flow Rate Measurement: Useful or Marketing Feature?

The Basic 2.0's water flow rate display is genuinely useful if you understand what to do with the data. It shows how fast water is passing through the puck during extraction , a slowdown mid-shot often indicates channeling, and a consistently fast flow across multiple shots suggests the grind is too coarse. This is live diagnostic data that used to require a separate flowmeter to capture. For buyers actively dialing in espresso, it shortens the feedback loop. For casual home brewers who pull the same recipe every morning without adjustment, it's a feature that will go unused. Worth having either way, since it's not an additional cost at the Basic 2.0 level , but set expectations appropriately.

The full range of tools that support a dialed-in espresso workflow is covered in the [coffee accessories](/accessories/) section, which puts the scale decision in context alongside grinders, distribution tools, and other setup components.

### Weight Capacity and Daily Use

All scales in this lineup handle standard espresso and single-serve pour-over without strain. The 2kg maximum on the Maestri House scale is the tightest constraint here; the Timemore models generally support higher maximum loads. If you're using the scale for kitchen tasks beyond coffee , bulk ingredient weighing, baking, batch recipe work , capacity matters. A scale used exclusively for coffee will never approach the capacity limit of any option in this group.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the TIMEMORE Basic 2.0 and the TIMEMORE Basic Mini?

The Basic 2.0 is the standard-profile model with a larger display and includes water flow rate measurement alongside the integrated timer. The Mini is a smaller, thinner version designed for machines with limited drip tray clearance. Both deliver solid espresso-scale performance, but the Basic 2.0 carries more features and a larger display, while the Mini prioritizes physical fit in constrained setups. The Mini does not include water flow rate measurement.
### Is the Maestri House scale accurate enough for espresso?

The [Maestri House Mini Coffee Scale with Timer](product:maestri-house-mini-coffee-scale-with-timer) is rated to 0.1g resolution, which is appropriate for espresso work. The 0.1g precision is sufficient for consistent dose measurement and yield tracking. It's not a Timemore, and it doesn't carry the same track record in espresso-focused communities, but the measurement performance on this unit holds up for daily home use.
### Does the TIMEMORE Basic 2.0 auto-tare when a cup is placed on it?

The Basic 2.0 supports manual tare via a single button press, which is standard for this scale category. Some higher-tier espresso scales include auto-tare triggered by weight detection, but the Basic 2.0 requires a deliberate tare operation. For most home espresso workflows this is not a practical limitation , taring before the shot is a one-tap step that adds negligible time to the routine.
### Can I use a mini coffee scale for pour-over as well as espresso?
Yes. The Mini form factor scales in this lineup , both the [TIMEMORE Basic Mini](product:timemore-basic-mini-coffee-scale-multifunction) and the [Maestri House](product:maestri-house-mini-coffee-scale-with-timer) , handle pour-over without issue. The integrated timer is useful for pour-over pacing as well as espresso timing. The only practical consideration is that a very small scale surface may feel cramped when positioning a larger pour-over vessel.
### How long do batteries last in a TIMEMORE coffee scale?

Battery life depends on usage frequency and the specific model. For daily espresso use , one to two shots per morning , expect two to four months from a standard set of AAA batteries. If battery management is a friction point for you, the [Maestri House Mini](product:maestri-house-mini-coffee-scale-with-timer) is the USB-C rechargeable option in this lineup and eliminates disposable battery replacement entirely.
