Holler Mountain is Stumptown's flagship blend, and if you've spent any time in the Portland coffee scene, you probably already know it. Whether you're comparing bag sizes, choosing between whole bean and pre-ground, or deciding whether Holler Mountain is actually the right roast for your setup, the options are more varied than they first appear. The full range of [Coffee Beans & Roasts](/beans/) is worth understanding before you settle on a bag.

What separates a good purchase from a frustrating one here isn't roast quality , Stumptown's sourcing is consistent , it's format, freshness, and fit. Whole bean or pre-ground, 12 ounce or 18 ounce, Holler Mountain or Hair Bender: each decision compounds.

## What to Look For in Coffee Beans & Roasts

### Roast Date vs. Best-By Date

The roast date is the most useful piece of information on a coffee bag, and a surprising number of roasters still bury it, omit it, or replace it with a "best by" date that tells you almost nothing. A best-by date typically runs 12 to 18 months from roasting. By the time you open that bag, the coffee could be eight months old and technically "fresh" by the label's standard.

Stumptown prints roast dates, which puts them ahead of most grocery-store options automatically. For espresso, I won't use beans beyond three weeks from roast. Pour-over is more forgiving , four to five weeks is workable , but the principle holds. Freshness isn't an audiophile affectation. CO2 off-gassing after roasting is real chemistry, and stale beans produce flat, hollow shots regardless of how well your machine is dialled in.

If you're buying online, factor in shipping and delivery time against the roast date. A bag roasted last Tuesday and delivered Friday is different from one roasted three weeks ago and sitting in a warehouse.

### Whole Bean vs. Pre-Ground

Pre-ground coffee oxidizes faster than whole beans. The surface area exposed to air increases enormously once you grind, and the aromatics that define a coffee's character , citrus brightness, caramel sweetness, hazelnut body , begin degrading within hours. For Holler Mountain specifically, the citrus zest note is the first thing to go in pre-ground.

Whole bean is the better choice if you have a grinder or are willing to buy one. A mid-range burr grinder , Baratza Encore, Niche Zero if you're going deeper , will do more for your cup than any bag upgrade. That said, pre-ground is not a catastrophic compromise if freshness is managed: buy smaller bags, use them within two weeks of opening, and store in an airtight container away from light.

The pre-ground format exists because it's convenient, and convenience matters. Just go in with clear expectations about what you're trading.

### Bag Size and Consumption Rate

An 18-ounce bag sounds like good value relative to 12 ounces. Whether it is depends entirely on your weekly consumption and how fast you move through the coffee. A single daily pourover uses roughly 20, 25 grams. At that rate, an 18-ounce bag lasts about three weeks , right at the edge of the freshness window for espresso, still fine for pour-over.

If you're brewing for two people or running a moka pot every morning, 18 ounces disappears in less than two weeks and the value calculation improves. If you brew occasionally, the 12-ounce bag is smarter: less coffee sitting in the pantry going stale.

Before exploring the full range of [whole bean and ground options](/beans/), be honest about your actual consumption. Buying coffee optimized for freshness only works if the bag size matches your pace.

### Roast Level and Brewing Method

Holler Mountain is a medium roast, which makes it legitimately versatile. It works in drip machines, pour-over, French press, and as an espresso base. The citrus zest and caramel notes read differently depending on brew method , brighter and more distinct in pour-over, rounder and more integrated in espresso.

Medium roast means you get both development from the roasting process and origin character from the bean. A lighter roast would push the citrus notes further forward; a darker roast would suppress them in favor of more bitter, roasted flavors. For most home setups, medium is the practical center.

Hair Bender, Stumptown's other widely available blend, skews slightly toward espresso use. Holler Mountain is the more general-purpose choice.

## Buying Guide

### Who Should Buy Holler Mountain vs. Another Stumptown Blend

Holler Mountain is Stumptown's general-purpose answer , medium roast, well-balanced, designed to work across brew methods. Most buyers landing on this page are in the right place. If you're running a drip machine, a pour-over, or a French press, Holler Mountain is the correct starting point in Stumptown's lineup.

Where it becomes a more considered decision: espresso. Holler Mountain works in espresso, but Hair Bender is better suited to it. If your primary brewing method is a machine pulling shots, Hair Bender is worth the comparison. If you're making a mix of drinks , morning pour-over, occasional espresso-based milk drink , Holler Mountain's versatility wins.

### Whole Bean vs. Pre-Ground for Your Specific Setup

The choice between whole bean and pre-ground should be determined by what you actually have in your kitchen, not what you aspire to own. If you have a burr grinder and use it consistently, whole bean is the obvious answer. If you have a blade grinder or no grinder at all, the pre-ground 12-ounce bag is not a step down in Stumptown's quality , it's a practical accommodation of your setup.

The version of this advice that's usually omitted: a mediocre grind on whole beans can be worse than a quality pre-ground. A blade grinder produces inconsistent particle sizes that extract unevenly. If your only option is a blade grinder, the pre-ground is genuinely competitive. The gap between pre-ground and whole bean narrows significantly when the grinder on the whole-bean side is poor.

### Bag Size Decision

The 18-ounce bag is better value per ounce, but that calculation only holds if you're moving through coffee fast enough that freshness isn't compromised. Run the numbers on your actual consumption before defaulting to the larger size. For households brewing one cup daily, the 12-ounce bag keeps you inside the freshness window more reliably.

For two or more daily drinkers, or anyone running a moka pot, Aeropress, and pour-over out of the same bag, 18 ounces makes sense. For solo brewers, the 12-ounce format is the honest choice , even if it feels like less value per purchase.

### Roast Date and Online Ordering

This matters more for online purchases than in-store. Specialty coffee retailers like Stumptown typically turn inventory quickly, but warehouse dwell time is a variable you can't control. When the bag arrives, check the roast date immediately. If you're outside a three-week window for espresso use, or you're seeing dates that suggest warehouse sitting time, that's feedback worth accounting for next time , whether it means ordering elsewhere, ordering more frequently in smaller batches, or adjusting how you prioritize freshness versus convenience.

The full spectrum of freshness-conscious [coffee beans and roasts](/beans/) is worth reviewing if Stumptown's online lead times are inconsistent in your region. Other specialty roasters operate direct-to-consumer models that roast to order, which eliminates the warehouse variable entirely.

### Storage After Opening

Whatever format you buy, storage after opening directly affects how long the bag stays useful. Exposure to air, light, heat, and moisture all accelerate staling. The resealable bags most specialty roasters use are adequate for a week or two. Beyond that, a dedicated airtight canister , not the refrigerator, not the freezer for beans you're using immediately , is the practical upgrade.

Freezing is viable only for beans you won't open for weeks. Freezing and thawing repeatedly introduces condensation, which is worse than ambient air storage. If you buy in bulk or want to extend the life of an 18-ounce bag you're moving through slowly, portion the beans into smaller airtight containers before freezing and thaw only what you'll use within a week.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Holler Mountain and Hair Bender?

Holler Mountain is Stumptown's versatile everyday blend , medium roast, citrus zest, caramel, and hazelnut, built to work across drip, pour-over, and French press. Hair Bender is tuned more specifically toward espresso, with citrus and dark chocolate notes that hold up under pressure extraction. For most home brewers without a dedicated espresso machine, [Stumptown Holler Mountain](product:stumptown-coffee-roasters-medium-roast-organic-whole-bean) is the more practical choice. If you pull shots daily, Hair Bender is worth comparing directly.
### Is pre-ground Holler Mountain worth buying if I don't own a grinder?

Yes, with managed expectations. The [Stumptown Holler Mountain pre-ground](product:stumptown-coffee-roasters-organic-medium-roast-ground-coffee) loses some of the citrus brightness that makes this blend distinct, because oxidation starts as soon as coffee is ground. Open the bag, use it within two weeks, and store it in an airtight container. The convenience is real, and the quality is still well above most supermarket options at the same format.
### Should I buy the 12-ounce or 18-ounce whole bean bag?

That depends on your weekly consumption rate. A single daily brew uses roughly 20, 25 grams, which means an 18-ounce bag lasts about three weeks , right at the edge of the freshness window for espresso. For one-cup-a-day drinkers, the 12-ounce bag keeps you inside a reliable freshness range. For households with two or more daily drinkers, the [18-ounce whole bean bag](product:stumptown-coffee-roasters-medium-roast-organic-whole-bean) makes practical and economic sense.
### How do I know how fresh the Stumptown coffee is when it arrives?

Check the roast date printed on the bag immediately on arrival , Stumptown includes this, which is better practice than most large-scale roasters. For espresso, three weeks from roast is a reasonable ceiling. Pour-over allows a slightly wider window, around four to five weeks. If the roast date suggests the bag spent significant time in transit or warehousing, factor that into your expectations and adjust brew method accordingly.
### Can I use Holler Mountain for espresso?
Yes. Holler Mountain works as espresso and produces a balanced shot with caramel sweetness and hazelnut body. It is not, however, specifically optimised for espresso the way [Hair Bender](product:stumptown-coffee-roasters-medium-roast-whole-bean-coffee) is. If espresso is your primary brew method and you're dialling in a recipe, Hair Bender will give you more to work with in terms of flavor definition under extraction.
