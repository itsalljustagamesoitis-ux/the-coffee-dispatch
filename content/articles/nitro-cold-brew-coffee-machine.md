Nitro cold brew at home sounds simple until you start researching the equipment. The category splits into two genuinely different approaches , nitrogen keg systems that use gas cartridges to infuse and dispense, and electric air-powered machines that skip the cartridges entirely , and choosing the wrong type means either ongoing consumable costs or a workflow that doesn't match how you actually use your kitchen. A quick scan of the [Coffee Makers](/coffee-makers/) category makes clear this isn't a niche corner of the market anymore.

The five machines below cover both approaches across a range of capacities. I've structured this to help you identify which system fits your household before you commit.

## What to Look For in a Nitro Cold Brew Coffee Machine

### Infusion Method: Nitrogen Cartridges vs. Air-Powered Systems

The most consequential decision in this category isn't brand , it's infusion technology. Traditional nitro cold brew systems use pressurized nitrogen gas, either from small cartridges or a larger tank, to push cold brew through a stout tap and create that characteristic cascading pour and creamy mouthfeel. The nitrogen dissolves into the liquid, producing a denser texture and suppressing the perception of acidity. The tradeoff is that you're buying consumables indefinitely. Cartridges are inexpensive individually but add up over months of regular use, and running out mid-week is genuinely annoying.

Air-powered systems, the newer approach, use an electric pump to infuse the beverage with pressurized air rather than pure nitrogen. The practical result is close , you get the creamy texture and reduced bite , but the long-term running cost drops significantly. No cartridges, no tanks to refill. For daily use, that distinction matters. For occasional use or gifting, a nitrogen keg system is more dramatic to look at and easier to explain.

### Capacity and Brewing Frequency

Cold brew concentrate takes 12, 24 hours to steep regardless of which system you're using. That means capacity is really a question of how often you want to run a batch. A 64 oz keg gives a moderate household enough cold brew for roughly five to seven days of daily pours, assuming a standard 8, 10 oz serving with some dilution. The 128 oz option essentially halves your brewing frequency , useful if you're serving a household of several coffee drinkers or hosting regularly.

Smaller-capacity electric machines force more frequent brewing but integrate into a standard countertop routine more easily. If you brew coffee on a schedule anyway, the more frequent cycle isn't a burden. If you'd rather set it and forget it for a week, capacity matters more than convenience features.

### Build Quality and Long-Term Maintenance

Stainless steel construction is the right call for anything that's going to live on your counter and contact cold brew repeatedly. Cold brew is more acidic than it tastes, and plastic components in contact with the liquid for extended periods raise legitimate questions about off-flavors and durability. Look for sealed gaskets and replaceable o-rings , these are the components that wear first in any pressurized system, and a machine without accessible service parts has a limited useful life.

For keg-style systems, the faucet assembly deserves specific attention. A stout tap that seals properly between pours keeps oxidation out and maintains the nitrogen charge in the headspace. Sloppy faucet tolerances are the most common complaint in this category, and they're rarely obvious from product photos.

### Versatility and Intended Use

Some machines in this category position themselves as multi-function beverage systems , nitro coffee, cocktails, matcha. That's a reasonable pitch if your household actually makes those things, but worth interrogating honestly. A machine optimized for cold brew brewing and dispensing is going to do that one thing better than a machine trying to serve six use cases.

The honest question is whether you'll use the extra functions or whether they're selling features you'll ignore. Exploring the full range of [coffee maker options](/coffee-makers/) before settling on a nitro system is worth the time , there are excellent cold brew setups that don't require pressurized gas at all, and for some households they're the better answer.

## Buying Guide

### Nitrogen Keg Systems vs. Electric Air-Powered Machines

The Royal Brew lineup uses pressurized nitrogen cartridges. The NitroPress machines use electric air pumps. This is the single most important variable in this purchase. Nitrogen cartridge systems are simple, mechanical, and reliable , the technology is proven, service parts are widely available, and the pour is genuinely theatrical. Electric air-powered systems are more convenient for daily use, produce consistent infusion without technique variability, and eliminate ongoing gas costs.

For light or occasional use , a batch once or twice a month, a gift purchase , the nitrogen keg approach is appropriate. For daily use over a year or more, the cartridge costs and logistics of the keg system accumulate in ways that matter.

### Capacity for Your Household

Match capacity to actual consumption, not to the largest option available. A 64 oz keg brewed weekly covers most one- or two-person households comfortably. A 128 oz keg is appropriate when you're serving three or more daily drinkers or want to extend the brewing cycle significantly.

Oversizing creates its own problem: cold brew doesn't improve with extended storage, and a half-full nitrogen keg loses its charge integrity faster than a full one. Larger batches also mean larger initial nitrogen charges and more gas consumed per cycle. The right size is the one that gets fully consumed before quality degrades , typically within ten to fourteen days for best results.

### The Consumables Calculation

Anyone buying a nitrogen cartridge-based system should run the numbers honestly before committing. Cartridges are inexpensive individually, but frequent use adds up. If you're brewing twice a week and using one or two cartridges per batch, the annual cost of consumables is not trivial. That cost belongs in the total cost of ownership, not just the purchase price.

Electric systems eliminate this entirely. If ongoing gas costs concern you, the NitroPress models remove that variable from the equation. The [coffee makers](/coffee-makers/) category more broadly has options that skip pressurized infusion altogether , regular cold brew makers that produce excellent concentrate without any gas system , and for budget-conscious buyers, that's worth knowing.

### Maintenance and Service Life

Any pressurized system requires maintenance to stay performing well. For nitrogen keg systems, the faucet assembly and keg seals are the primary wear components. O-rings compress and degrade over time, and a keg that won't hold pressure is a keg that delivers flat, oxidized cold brew. Replacement parts should be available and inexpensive before you buy.

Electric systems have their own maintenance considerations , pump longevity and electrical components are the variables here. Stainless steel construction throughout is the baseline requirement for anything in regular beverage contact. Check that cleaning is straightforward: complex internal geometries that trap liquid are a persistent source of off-flavors in machines that can't be fully dried between uses.

### Storage and Counter Footprint

A 128 oz keg is a substantial object. A 64 oz keg is more manageable but still requires dedicated counter space. Neither is designed for refrigerator storage, which means ambient temperature affects cold brew quality over the storage period. In a warm kitchen, this shortens the useful window meaningfully.

Electric machines tend toward a more compact footprint that integrates into a standard coffee station. If counter space is constrained, that's a real practical advantage. Before buying any keg system, measure your counter depth and overhead clearance , keg faucets require vertical clearance to pour comfortably, which isn't obvious from product listings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a nitrogen cartridge system and an electric air-powered nitro machine?

Nitrogen cartridge systems, like the Royal Brew kegs, use pressurized N₂ gas to infuse cold brew and push it through a stout tap , the same mechanism used in commercial nitro coffee bars. Electric air-powered systems, like the NitroPress machines, use an internal pump to achieve similar infusion without disposable cartridges. The texture and pour are comparable, but the electric approach eliminates ongoing cartridge costs and removes the logistics of keeping gas on hand.
### Is the Royal Brew 64 oz or 128 oz keg the better choice for a home setup?

For a single person or a couple, the 64 oz keg is the practical choice , it produces enough cold brew for a full week without waste and keeps the batch fresh. The 128 oz option makes sense for households of three or more regular cold brew drinkers, or for anyone who wants to extend the brewing cycle to every two weeks. The larger keg also requires more nitrogen per charge and more counter space, so those are real tradeoffs.
### Do nitro cold brew machines brew the cold brew concentrate, or do they just infuse it?

Most machines in this category , including all five reviewed here , infuse and dispense cold brew that you've already steeped separately. You steep ground coffee in cold water for 12, 24 hours using a standard cold brew setup, filter it, then transfer the concentrate to the machine for nitrogen infusion. The machine is responsible for the nitro texture, not the brewing itself. Some electric systems offer more integrated brewing functionality, but the infusion step is still the primary function.
### How often do nitrogen cartridges need to be replaced in a keg system?

Typically one to two cartridges per batch, depending on keg size and desired pressure. A full 64 oz charge usually requires one cartridge to get the system to serving pressure; a 128 oz keg may need two. You'll also re-charge between pours if the pressure drops noticeably. Cartridges are widely available and inexpensive individually, but if you're brewing several times a week, the annual cost adds up enough to factor into your decision between a cartridge system and an electric alternative like the [NitroPress DS](product:nitropress-ds-electric-nitro-cold).
### Can nitro cold brew machines be used to make anything other than coffee?

The NitroPress Pro and DS are explicitly designed for cocktails and other beverages alongside cold brew, and they deliver on that. The Royal Brew keg systems are optimized for cold brew dispensing , you can technically use them for other cold beverages, but they're not positioned or optimized for cocktail infusion. If multi-beverage functionality is genuinely useful to your household, the electric NitroPress machines are the appropriate choice. If you want a dedicated cold brew appliance with minimal complexity, the keg systems are cleaner for that single purpose.
