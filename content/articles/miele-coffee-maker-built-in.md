Miele's built-in coffee machines occupy a specific category of kitchen appliance , one that rewards buyers who understand exactly what they're buying and punishes those who don't. These are [Coffee Makers](/coffee-makers/) in the super-automatic tradition: bean-to-cup convenience appliances that grind, brew, and in some cases froth milk at the press of a button. If that's what you want, Miele builds some of the best.

What separates a good Miele purchase from a regrettable one is knowing which machine fits your kitchen, your household, and your actual habits , and understanding that the accessories you pair with it are not optional extras.

## What to Look For in a Miele Built-In Coffee Maker

### Machine Tier and Feature Set

Miele's automatic coffee machine lineup is tiered, and the differences between tiers are real. The lower models handle straightforward brewing well. The upper models add milk system integration, more granular profile customization, connectivity features, and , in some cases , the ability to brew two drinks simultaneously without pressing a button twice. That last feature sounds minor until you've made coffee for a household where two people want their drinks at the same time every morning.

The question to ask is not which machine has the most features, but which features you'll actually use after the first month. AromaticSystem brewing and individual profile settings are genuinely useful if multiple people in the household drink coffee differently. Built-in milk frothing saves counter space and simplifies the morning routine. WiFi compatibility is nice but rarely changes the quality of the drink.

### Milk System Integration

Not all Miele machines handle milk the same way. Some have integrated milk containers that sit inside the machine or connect directly to it. Others require you to position a separate carafe. The distinction matters for counter organization and for how quickly the system can deliver a milk-based drink without manual intervention.

If your household runs primarily on flat whites, cappuccinos, or lattes, a machine with a proper integrated milk system is worth prioritizing. If you drink mostly black coffee with milk-based drinks as an occasional addition, a simpler setup is fine. The MilkPerfection system on the upper-tier machines is genuinely capable , but only relevant if milk drinks are a regular part of the routine.

### Maintenance Commitment

This is where many buyers underestimate the total cost of ownership of an automatic coffee machine. Miele machines are well-engineered, but they are not self-maintaining. Descaling is required on a schedule that depends on your water hardness. Cleaning tablets handle milk system residue and brewing group buildup. Ignoring these cycles degrades brew quality noticeably and, over time, shortens machine life.

Miele makes its own descaling tablets, cleaning tablets, and descaling cartridges , and for their machines, using the genuine articles is the correct call. The machines are designed with Miele's own consumables in mind. Stocking up at purchase rather than scrambling when the machine flags a cleaning cycle is a better habit to build from the start. A full range of [coffee makers and accessories](/coffee-makers/) from Miele is worth reviewing before you commit to a setup.

### Profile and Customization Depth

Individual profile settings are one of the features that distinguish Miele from commodity super-automatics. The ability to store a specific grind level, brew strength, temperature, and volume for each user means that the machine can produce consistent results across different household members without anyone re-adjusting the settings each time.

For a single-person household, this matters less. For a household of three or four adults who all drink coffee differently, it matters considerably. Consider whether the profile depth of a given model matches the complexity of your household's actual coffee preferences.

### Built-In vs. Countertop Placement

"Built-in" in Miele's terminology can mean a few things: some models are designed to integrate flush into cabinetry with a niche install, while others sit on a counter but are marketed under the same product family. If you're planning a true niche installation , common in kitchen renovations , confirm the specific model's installation requirements before purchasing. A machine specified for niche installation needs adequate ventilation and access for maintenance. Getting that wrong is an expensive correction.

## Buying Guide

### Super-Automatic vs. Everything Else

The most important framing for this purchase is categorical. Miele's built-in coffee machines are super-automatics , convenience appliances that automate the grinding, dosing, tamping, and brewing process entirely. They are not manual espresso machines that make espresso badly. They are a different category of product doing a different job.

If you want control over extraction variables , grind size, dose, tamp pressure, shot time , a super-automatic will frustrate you. If you want consistent, hands-off coffee at the press of a button, a super-automatic done well is genuinely excellent. Buy for what you actually want, not for what sounds more serious.

### Which Machine for Which Household

The CM 6360 is the right machine for households with multiple coffee drinkers who want milk-based drinks as part of a daily routine. The eight profiles, DoubleShot functionality, and MilkPerfection system add up to a meaningfully better experience for that specific use case.

The CM 5310 is the right machine for households that want Miele's build quality and profile customization without paying for a milk system they won't fully use. The operational noise reduction is a genuine differentiator in open kitchens. The full range of [Miele coffee makers](/coffee-makers/) is worth reviewing side by side to confirm which tier fits your situation before purchasing.

### Maintenance Is Not Optional

The clearest mistake buyers make with automatic coffee machines is treating the maintenance consumables as optional. They are not. Miele's machines will flag descaling and cleaning cycles explicitly , the machine tells you when it needs attention. Running those cycles on schedule with the correct Miele tablets and cartridges preserves brew quality and machine longevity. Delaying them degrades both.

Budget for consumables at the time of purchase, not retroactively. A year's supply of descaling tablets and cleaning tablets is a small cost relative to the machine price. Having them on hand removes the friction that causes people to skip cycles.

### Installation and Kitchen Integration

True built-in installation requires planning. Adequate ventilation, access for the water reservoir and bean hopper, and clearance for the drip tray all need to be accounted for in the cabinetry design. A machine installed without sufficient ventilation or in an awkward position for daily access becomes a friction point that erodes how much it gets used.

If you are doing a kitchen renovation and plan to integrate the machine into cabinetry, involve a kitchen designer who has worked with Miele installations specifically. The dimensional tolerances are exact, and retrofitting a cabinet opening that's even slightly wrong is a meaningful additional cost.

### Grinder Considerations for Super-Automatics

Miele's super-automatics have built-in grinders, which is part of what makes them convenient. The grinders are adequate for the category , they produce a consistent enough grind for the machine's brewing parameters. They are not, however, the variable you can optimize the way you can with a standalone grinder on a manual setup.

For buyers coming from a manual espresso background, this is the main performance ceiling of the super-automatic format. The machine controls the grind. You adjust within the parameters it allows, not outside them. For buyers who want bean-to-cup convenience without managing a separate grinder, this is precisely the point , it is a feature, not a limitation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the Miele CM 6360 and CM 5310?

The CM 6360 adds the MilkPerfection integrated milk system, eight individual user profiles, DoubleShot dual-cup functionality, and WiFi compatibility. The CM 5310 handles standard brewing with profile settings and OneTouch for Two but lacks the full milk system integration. If milk-based drinks are a daily priority for multiple household members, the CM 6360 is worth the step up. If black coffee and simple Americanos dominate, the CM 5310 covers the use case without the added complexity.
### Do Miele coffee machines require special cleaning products?

Miele strongly recommends using their own consumables , the [Miele Descaling Tablets](product:miele-descaling-tablets-for-coffee-machines) and [Miele Coffee Machine Cleaning Tablets](product:miele-coffee-machine-cleaning-tablets-20) , in their machines. The cleaning chemistry is calibrated for Miele's internal components, and using off-brand alternatives can void warranty coverage and, in some cases, cause residue buildup that affects taste. The tablets are a recurring consumable cost worth factoring into the total ownership budget from the start.
### Are Miele built-in coffee machines true espresso machines?
No , and that distinction matters. Miele's automatic machines produce an espresso-adjacent drink using a pressurized brewing system. They lack the manual control over extraction variables that a dedicated espresso machine offers. For buyers who want consistent, convenient coffee at the push of a button, Miele's machines are very good at what they do.
### How often does a Miele coffee machine need descaling?
Descaling frequency depends on water hardness and how heavily the machine is used. Most Miele machines have a water hardness setting that adjusts the descaling interval accordingly , softer water requires less frequent descaling. In a typical household with moderate water hardness and daily use, descaling every two to three months is common. The machine will flag when a descaling cycle is required.
### Can a Miele coffee machine be installed in a cabinet niche?

Yes, and many of Miele's models are specifically designed for niche installation to integrate flush with cabinetry. The key requirements are adequate ventilation around the machine, easy access to the water reservoir and bean hopper for daily use, and clearance for the drip tray removal. Check the specific model's installation specifications , they vary between the CM 5310 and CM 6360 , and have a kitchen designer review the dimensions before the cabinetry is built.
