Mazzer makes some of the most respected grinders in the commercial espresso world, but most buyers searching that name end up comparing a much wider field , budget electrics, mid-range flat burrs, prosumer stepless grinders , and need a clear guide through it. These five picks cover the realistic range a home buyer considers once they start looking seriously at [Coffee Grinders](/grinders/).

The category is unforgiving. Burr geometry, retention, and grind consistency separate a grinder that improves your coffee from one that just spins the beans. I've owned enough to know where the real differences lie, and I'll be direct about which picks earn their place and which are here as honest context.

## What to Look For in a Coffee Grinder

### Burr Type and What It Actually Means

Flat burrs and conical burrs are the two designs you'll encounter, and they behave differently in ways that matter depending on how you brew. Flat burrs cut parallel to each other, producing a narrower grind distribution , most particles land close to your target size. That consistency is why flat burrs dominate the commercial espresso world.

Conical burrs orient at an angle, which tends to produce a bimodal distribution: a concentration of particles at your target size plus a secondary cluster of fines. For pour over and immersion brews, those fines can actually add body. Neither is universally better , the question is which matches how you brew.

What you're trying to avoid entirely is a blade grinder. I used one for two weeks before I understood why my espresso was undrinkable. A blade doesn't grind , it chops, producing a mix of powder and chunks that guarantees uneven extraction. Even the most modest burr grinder is a categorical improvement. There is no workaround for a blade.

### Grind Adjustment: Stepped, Stepless, and How Fine the Steps Are

Stepped grinders move between fixed positions. The question is how many positions exist and how far apart they are , 48 steps across the full range gives you more precision than 10, but the gaps still matter. If your espresso target falls between two clicks, you can't split the difference.

Stepless grinders let you set any position within the full range, which makes dialling in espresso considerably more tractable. The trade-off is that repeating a setting from memory requires a reference mark or written note rather than a click count. For pour over and filter work, where tolerances are more forgiving, a 40-to-48-step grinder is plenty.

### Retention and Why It Matters More Than Most Reviews Acknowledge

Retention is the weight of coffee ground on one dose that stays inside the grinder and comes out with the next dose. High-retention grinders waste your best beans. If you're buying specialty coffee at premium prices, you're discarding some of it with every grind change , and you're also introducing stale grounds from the previous session.

For single-dose work, the lowest-retention grinders hold under half a gram; the worst hold several grams. That math compounds fast. The best electric grinders at every price point now treat low retention as a design priority rather than an afterthought, and you should weigh it accordingly when choosing. Browsing the full range of [grinder options](/grinders/) before settling on a format is worth doing , the retention spec is often buried in the listing but it belongs near the top of your checklist.

### Cleaning and Long-Term Maintenance

Burrs collect coffee oils and fine particles between cleaning sessions. If you grind every day and never clean, shot consistency degrades and rancid oil flavors transfer to fresh beans. How easy the grinder makes cleaning , whether the burrs are accessible, whether the hopper detaches cleanly, whether the manufacturer provides guidance , matters more over three years than it does in the first week.

Dedicated cleaning tablets exist for a reason. They're more thorough than a brush and more practical than full disassembly. The ongoing cost is real but minor against the cost of the grinder and the beans. Treat cleaning as part of owning a grinder, not an optional extra.

## Top Picks

### Fellow Ode Gen 2 64mm

The [Fellow Ode Gen 2](product:fellow-ode-gen-2-64mm) is the clearest recommendation for a home brewer who cares about filter coffee , pour over, French press, cold brew , and wants a grinder that's been engineered rather than assembled. The 64mm flat burr set produces a consistent grind distribution across its 31 settings, and the single-dose design means you grind what you use and nothing sits in a hopper going stale.

Retention on the Gen 2 is low enough that I consider it a genuine feature rather than a marketing claim. Single-dose grinders live or die by this number, and Fellow's second-generation design addressed the retention issues that plagued the original. The anti-static treatment makes the grounds transfer from the catch cup without static cling, which is a small thing until you've dealt with the alternative.

The one honest caveat: the Ode is designed for filter, not espresso. The 31 settings don't include the fine end of the range where espresso lives, and Fellow is transparent about this. If you split your time between filter and espresso, this isn't the right tool. If filter is your method, it's the best option at this tier.

For a buyer who wants one grinder that handles espresso seriously and filter competently, the Sculptor 064S is where I'd point them. TIMEMORE's build quality has been consistently better than their price point suggests, and the 064S continues that trend.

## Buying Guide

### Filter Versus Espresso: The First Question

Most grinders optimize for one brewing method better than another, even when marketed as all-purpose. Filter brewing , pour over, French press, cold brew , is tolerant of a wider grind distribution and doesn't require the fine, precise control that espresso demands. Espresso grinders need to operate at finer settings with more granular adjustment because a small shift in grind size has an outsized effect on extraction pressure and shot time.

If you brew only filter, the Fellow Ode Gen 2 is the clear pick. If you pull espresso, the TIMEMORE Sculptor 064S earns the recommendation. If you genuinely do both in roughly equal measure, a conical burr grinder with a wide setting range , like the Tuni G1 , is a reasonable compromise, with the understanding that compromises are real.

### Single Dose Versus Hopper Feeding

Single-dose grinders hold no coffee in reserve , you weigh your beans, pour them in, and grind the dose. Hopper-fed grinders keep a supply of beans loaded and grind on demand, which is faster for high-volume use but introduces two problems for the home user: stale beans sitting in the hopper and cross-contamination when you switch coffees.

For home use with specialty coffee, single-dose is almost always the right format. You buy better beans in smaller quantities, you want each bag to taste like itself, and you're not pulling twenty shots in sequence. The Fellow Ode Gen 2 was designed with single-dose as the primary use case, and it shows in the low retention spec.

### Retention: What the Number Actually Tells You

Retention figures in grinder specs tell you how much coffee stays inside the machine between doses. Low retention , under 0.5 grams , means what you grind is what you get. Higher retention means your first dose after a grind setting change contains old grounds, and your expensive beans are partially subsidizing the next session's puck.

The [grinder category](/grinders/) has moved meaningfully on retention in the past few years, and it's now a spec worth demanding rather than accepting. If a listing doesn't mention retention, that's useful information. Check the grinder community forums , retention is one of the most consistently tested variables among home users, and real-world numbers exist for most popular models.

### Build Quality and Expected Lifespan

A burr grinder is a mechanical device with tolerances that degrade over time if the construction isn't solid. Motor quality, burr material (steel versus ceramic), housing rigidity, and the quality of the grind adjustment mechanism all affect how long the grinder maintains its initial performance. A grinder that's accurate on day one but drifts over a year of use isn't worth its purchase price.

At the premium end of the home market, burrs are typically rated for hundreds of kilograms of coffee before meaningful wear. Budget grinders don't publish these figures because the numbers aren't favorable. Ask how the manufacturer supports the product if a component fails , whether replacement burrs are available, whether service is accessible. That question alone sorts the field considerably.

### Cleaning Frequency and Effort

Establishing a cleaning schedule matters more than the specific product you use to clean. The general guidance is a brush wipe of the burr chamber every week for daily users, and a full cleaning tablet run every two to four weeks depending on usage volume. Grinders with accessible burr chambers make this less onerous , if disassembly requires a toolkit, the cleaning gets skipped.

Urnex Grindz is the professional standard because it works consistently and the protocol is simple enough that you'll actually follow it. The food-safe, all-natural formulation means there's no chemical concern about residue in your coffee after the purge step. Maintenance done regularly and simply beats infrequent thorough cleaning every time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a Mazzer grinder worth buying for home use?

Mazzer's commercial grinders , the Mini, the Major, the Robur , are built for café throughput and priced accordingly. For home use, the throughput capacity is unnecessary and the retail price is hard to justify unless you're pulling ten or more shots daily. Most buyers searching for Mazzer end up better served by home-focused alternatives like the TIMEMORE Sculptor 064S, which delivers flat burr precision at a price scaled to home use rather than commercial volume.
### What is the difference between flat burr and conical burr grinders?

Flat burrs cut parallel to each other and produce a narrower, more uniform particle distribution , a characteristic that benefits espresso extraction. Conical burrs orient at an angle, producing a wider distribution with more fines, which some brewers find adds body to filter cups. For espresso, flat burrs like those in the [Fellow Ode Gen 2](product:fellow-ode-gen-2-64mm) or TIMEMORE Sculptor generally produce more consistent results. For filter-only brewing, either design is viable, and the choice often comes down to other factors like retention and adjustment range.
### How often should I clean my burr grinder?

For daily use, a light brush of the burr chamber weekly and a cleaning tablet run every two to four weeks is a reasonable schedule. Coffee oils accumulate on burr surfaces and degrade flavor over time , rancid oil from weeks of buildup transfers directly to fresh beans. [Urnex Grindz](product:urnex-grindz-professional-coffee-grinder) tablets are the professional standard because the process is fast, thorough, and requires no disassembly. Neglecting cleaning is one of the most common reasons grinder performance deteriorates before the hardware actually wears out.
### Can I use one grinder for both espresso and filter brewing?

Yes, but with trade-offs. Grinders optimized for espresso , particularly those with stepless adjustment , can often run coarse enough for filter, though they may not be as accurate at the coarser end of their range. Grinders optimized for filter, like the Fellow Ode Gen 2, deliberately exclude the fine settings espresso requires. The Tuni G1's conical burr and 48-step range makes it a reasonable dual-purpose option, though a buyer who is serious about espresso will eventually want a dedicated espresso grinder.
### What does grinder retention mean and why does it matter?

Retention is the weight of ground coffee that stays inside the grinder between doses rather than exiting into your cup. High retention means you're not getting the full dose you ground, and stale grounds from a previous session contaminate your fresh dose. For specialty coffee, where you're grinding small quantities of carefully sourced beans, high retention is a real loss. Single-dose grinders like the Fellow Ode Gen 2 are designed specifically to minimize this , it's one of the most practically important specs to check before buying.
