French press glass breaks. It's not a question of whether you'll crack a beaker , it's whether it happens on a Tuesday morning when you're already running late, or on a weekend when you have time to deal with it. The good news is that a replacement beaker costs a fraction of a new press, and with [Brewing Methods](/brewing-methods/) options ranging from simple to obsessive, keeping your French press in service is almost always the right call.

The harder question is which replacement fits. Not every beaker drops into every frame, and the material choice , glass versus stainless , matters more than most buyers realize.

## What to Look For in a French Press Replacement Beaker

### Compatibility First, Everything Else Second

The most common mistake people make buying a replacement beaker is assuming "34 oz" is a universal fit. It isn't. French press frames vary by brand in their collar diameter, the height of the frame walls, and the geometry of the base. A beaker that holds the right volume can still be too narrow at the shoulder to seat properly in your frame, or too tall for the lid to close.

Before buying, measure your existing beaker if it's intact , or note the brand and model of your press frame. Most major brands (Bodum, Frieling, Espro) have proprietary collar dimensions. Some replacement beakers are engineered to fit a range of frames; others are brand-specific. When a product claims "universal fit," read that as "fits many common frames" rather than "fits all frames." The claim is usually good enough for popular brands but not guaranteed for obscure ones.

If you're buying blind , you don't know your frame brand , measure the inner diameter of the collar and the depth of the frame before ordering.

### Borosilicate Glass vs. Standard Glass vs. Stainless Steel

All three materials are represented in this category, and the tradeoffs are real.

Standard glass is cheaper to manufacture and to buy, but it has a lower thermal shock tolerance , pour boiling water into a cold standard glass beaker quickly enough and you're replacing it again sooner than you'd like. Borosilicate glass handles thermal cycling better. Fire-hardened borosilicate is the better version of that: the annealing process reduces internal stress points, making the glass more resistant to the rapid temperature changes that French press brewing involves.

Stainless steel is in a different category. It won't crack, won't shatter if you knock it off the counter, and holds heat longer than glass. The trade-off is that you can't see the brew. For experienced French press users who know their timing by habit, that's no loss. For people who watch the steep, it matters.

### Capacity and Serving Count

French press replacement beakers are overwhelmingly sold in two common capacities: 12 oz (roughly one to two servings) and 34 oz (roughly four to five servings, depending on your pour). The 34 oz size is often labeled as "8-cup" , that cup measurement refers to 4 oz servings, not the 8, 12 oz mugs most people actually use. A "34 oz / 8-cup" beaker will realistically fill three to four standard mugs.

The capacity you need is probably the same as your existing press, so measure before ordering if you're not sure. Buying a 12 oz replacement for a press that used to hold 34 oz will be an unpleasant surprise on a morning when four people want coffee.

### What "Fire-Hardened" Means in Practice

Fire hardening and borosilicate are frequently listed together, and they refer to different things. Borosilicate is a glass composition , lower expansion coefficient, better thermal shock resistance. Fire hardening (more precisely, thermal tempering) is a manufacturing process applied to the finished glass that further reduces the likelihood of breakage from impact or heat stress.

A fire-hardened borosilicate beaker is better than either attribute alone. For daily French press use , boiling water, repeated heating cycles, occasional countertop contact , it's the specification worth paying attention to. If a listing says only "glass beaker" without specifying composition or treatment, assume it's standard glass and buy accordingly.

## Buying Guide

### Fitting a Replacement Beaker to Your Frame

The fit question is the only one that can make an otherwise good beaker useless. French press frames hold the beaker at the collar , the metal or plastic ring that cinches around the top of the glass , and rest it in a base. The inner collar diameter and the base geometry vary enough between manufacturers that a beaker which fits a Bodum will not necessarily drop into a Frieling or a generic frame.

The safest approach: if you know your press brand and model, buy the brand's own spare part or a replacement explicitly listed as compatible. If you don't know the brand, measure the outer diameter of the broken beaker at the widest point. Universal-fit beakers are engineered around the most common collar dimensions, but "universal" covers a range, not every frame ever made.

### Glass Construction: What the Specs Actually Mean

Borosilicate glass is the minimum worth buying for French press use. The brewing process puts the glass through thermal cycling every day , cold glass, near-boiling water, then cooling on the counter. Standard glass handles that cycle poorly over time. Borosilicate handles it well. Fire-hardened borosilicate handles it better.

That said, no glass is drop-proof. Borosilicate resists thermal shock; it doesn't resist a hard impact with a stone countertop any better than standard glass does. If breakage is a recurring problem in your kitchen , small children, crowded prep space, a sink you reach awkwardly over , the material conversation leads to stainless, not to better glass.

### The Case for Stainless Over Glass

A stainless steel replacement carafe costs more upfront and eliminates the visual brew check, but it also eliminates the replacement cycle. One stainless carafe outlasts three or four glass beakers in a kitchen where glass breaks regularly.

The heat retention argument for stainless is real for long steeps or for people who get distracted and come back to their press after eight minutes. Glass loses heat faster; stainless keeps the brew temperature stable for longer. If you've ever had lukewarm French press because you forgot to push the plunger, that's a problem stainless solves.

The stainless option in this group , the [Fino 8-Cup Replacement Carafe](product:fino-8-cup-replacement-carafe) , is frame-specific, not universal. That narrows who should buy it, but for Fino press owners it's an unambiguous upgrade from glass.

### Capacity: Match Your Existing Press

Replacement beakers are not interchangeable across sizes even within the same brand. A 12 oz beaker does not fit in a 34 oz frame , the proportions are different, not just the volume. Buy the same capacity as your existing press.

The one exception: if you're replacing a press whose frame is adjustable or whose collar accepts multiple beaker heights, check the manufacturer's compatibility list. Most frames are not adjustable, and most buyers should just match the capacity. Among the French press methods covered across [Brewing Methods](/brewing-methods/), French press is one of the few formats where undersizing the vessel genuinely affects brew quality , too much headspace changes the crust formation and steep dynamics.

### Spare vs. Single Replacement

Buying two replacement beakers at once is worth considering if you brew daily and live somewhere where shipping takes more than a few days. One cracked beaker on a Monday morning means no French press until a replacement arrives , which, depending on your situation, might mean a week of inferior coffee.

A spare also enables the two-carafe workflow: brew in one, clean the other, switch. For people who clean their equipment immediately after use, it removes the friction of waiting for the beaker to cool and dry before the next brew.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a 34 oz replacement beaker fit any French press frame?

Not automatically. "34 oz" describes volume, not the collar diameter or base geometry that determines physical fit. A replacement labeled as "universal fit" covers most common frame dimensions , particularly major brands like Bodum, Frieling, and similar , but not every frame on the market. Measure your collar or check your press brand before ordering to avoid a return.
### Is borosilicate glass noticeably better than standard glass for French press use?
Yes, in one specific way that matters: thermal shock resistance. Boiling water poured into cold standard glass creates stress points that accumulate with use. Borosilicate handles that temperature transition reliably. Fire-hardened borosilicate is better still.
### Should I buy the Cafe Crush Club beaker or the Bruntmor if both fit my frame?

The practical differences are small. Both are fire-hardened borosilicate at 34 oz, and both will perform identically in daily use. The [French Press Replacement Glass by Cafe Crush Club](product:french-press-replacement-glass-by) has printed volume markings, which is a minor ergonomic advantage if you're still refining your dose-to-water ratio. If that feature is irrelevant to your workflow, buy whichever is in stock.
### What are the trade-offs of a stainless steel replacement carafe over glass?

Stainless doesn't break, holds heat longer through extended steeps, and eliminates the replacement cycle over time. The cost is losing visibility into the brew , you can't watch the steep or check color development. For experienced brewers working from a fixed recipe, that trade-off is nothing. For people who are still learning their timing, the visual feedback from glass is genuinely useful.
### Can I use a replacement beaker while my original is in the dishwasher?

Yes, and it's one of the better practical arguments for buying a spare. Borosilicate glass is generally dishwasher-safe , confirm this for your specific beaker, but most are , and having a second beaker means your brewing routine isn't interrupted by cleaning. The [Bodum 12oz Spare Carafe](product:bodum-12oz-spare-carafe-for) is particularly well-suited to this workflow for single-serve press owners.
