Coffee scales are one of those purchases that looks simple until you've owned a bad one. If you're serious about espresso or pour-over, you already know that [Accessories & Setup](/accessories/) extends well beyond your brewer , and a reliable scale is where precision brewing actually begins. The Timemore Black Mirror line has earned a genuine following in the specialty coffee world, but there are enough variants, and one capable alternative, that picking the right one takes more than reading the product title.

The differences between these scales are real but narrow. Flow rate, battery life, charging type, and platform size all matter depending on how you brew. Here's what to look for, and which one to buy.

## What to Look For in a Coffee Scale

### Precision and Resolution

The number most scale listings lead with is maximum capacity , 2kg is standard across this category and adequate for any home brewing scenario. The number that actually matters is resolution: 0.1g is the threshold for espresso work. A scale that reads in 0.5g increments is fine for pour-over but useless for dialing in a shot where the difference between 17.8g and 18.3g changes everything. Every scale in this roundup hits 0.1g, which is the baseline expectation for a specialty coffee scale in this range.

What the spec sheet won't tell you is response speed. Some 0.1g scales are so sensitive they take two or three seconds to settle on a final reading. For pour-over that's irrelevant. For espresso, where you're watching extraction in real time, a scale that lags becomes a liability. The Timemore platform has a reputation for faster response than many competitors at this weight class, which is part of why it dominates this category.

### Flow Rate Function

Flow rate measurement , grams per second displayed in real time , is the feature that separates a coffee scale from a kitchen scale wearing coffee-adjacent branding. For espresso, watching flow rate lets you identify under-extraction (too fast, too thin) and over-extraction (too slow, too much resistance) without waiting for the shot to finish. You can adjust grind between shots with actual data rather than taste and guesswork alone.

For pour-over, flow rate tells you whether your pouring technique is consistent. If you're targeting a specific brew ratio and noticing your 30-second intervals vary by 4g/s, your pour control , not your recipe , is the variable. The scales in this roundup that include flow rate are worth the marginal premium over those that don't.

### Battery and Charging

This matters more than it reads on a spec sheet. A scale that takes AA batteries is reliable in a narrow sense , it's never flat if you have spares , but adds recurring cost and produces waste. A scale with a proprietary charging cable is a small tax on your patience every time you're in a different room. USB-C is the right answer: universal, reversible, already in every cable drawer.

Battery capacity affects whether your scale survives a morning session without needing to return to the charger. Thin-profile scales sometimes compromise here. The 1600mAh cell in the Timemore Black Mirror Basic 2 is one of the more practical decisions Timemore made in that product line.

### Platform Size and Build

Espresso work needs a platform large enough to sit under a portafilter or a small cup without tipping. Pour-over demands enough surface for a Chemex or V60 without the vessel hanging off the edge. Compact scales solve a drawer-space problem but create a workflow problem if the platform is too small for your actual brewing setup.

The full range of [coffee brewing accessories](/accessories/) worth considering at this stage includes tampers, distribution tools, and dosing funnels , all of which live on or near your scale. Build quality matters too: a plastic-body scale that flexes when you set a moka pot on it will eventually affect readings.

## Buying Guide

### Which Timemore Variant Is Actually Different

The four Timemore products in this roundup share the same core specification , 0.1g resolution, 2kg capacity, timer, flow rate function. The meaningful differences are surface finish, physical protection, battery capacity, and charging. The silicone cover version offers genuine daily-use durability. The 1600mAh battery variant offers documented runtime. The mirror surface variant looks sharp but requires more cleaning. Knowing which of these variables matters to your setup is more useful than reading spec sheets closely, because the core specs are nearly identical across the line.

### Espresso vs. Pour-Over Use Cases

Espresso demands fast response time, reliable timer initiation, and a platform that fits under a portafilter without the scale shifting from vibration. Pour-over demands enough surface area for a Chemex or V60 and benefits more from auto-timer triggered by first weight contact. If you're exclusively pulling espresso shots, any of the Timemore variants will perform , but prioritize platform stability and timer control. If pour-over is the primary use, the auto-timer function on the [TIMEMORE Black Mirror Basic 2](product:timemore-black-mirror-basic-2-coffee-2) is specifically worth the consideration.

### Flow Rate: Essential or Optional

Flow rate display , grams per second in real time , is not a gimmick in this category. For espresso, it translates directly into actionable extraction data during the shot. For pour-over, it's a technique calibration tool. If you're early in the process of dialing in a recipe and grind setting, flow rate gives you information you'd otherwise have to infer after the fact. Every scale in this roundup includes it. If you're comparing these to budget kitchen scales without flow rate, that's the capability difference that justifies the step up. Browsing the broader [coffee accessories](/accessories/) category will surface alternatives, but flow rate is what separates this tier from general-purpose kitchen scales.

### Battery Life and Charging Practicality

A scale with a dead battery at 6:45am is useless. Battery capacity and charging type are worth taking seriously. USB-C, as found on the Maestri House, is the most convenient charging solution currently available in this category. Among the Timemore variants, the 1600mAh capacity on the Black Mirror Basic 2 is a documented runtime advantage. Charging habits vary , some people charge every night, some charge when the indicator appears. If you're the latter, higher capacity buys more margin before the scale goes flat mid-session.

### Platform Size and Your Actual Brewing Setup

Measure your largest brewing vessel before buying. A 40mm espresso cup and a Chemex have very different footprint requirements, and a scale that feels adequate in a product listing can feel cramped in practice. Compact scales like the Maestri House Mini solve a storage problem but create a workflow constraint for larger vessels. The Timemore platform dimensions sit in a comfortable middle range for most home setups. If counter space is the binding constraint, compact wins. If brewing variety is the goal, prioritize platform surface over overall scale footprint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between the TIMEMORE Black Mirror Basic 2 variants?
The core specification , 0.1g resolution, 2kg capacity, timer, and flow rate function , is shared across Timemore Black Mirror Basic 2 variants. The differences are surface treatment, physical protection, and battery specifications. The silicone cover version adds heat and moisture protection to the weighing platform. The 1600mAh variant emphasizes documented battery runtime.
### Is the Maestri House Mini a serious alternative to Timemore, or a budget fallback?

The Maestri House Mini is a genuine alternative for espresso-focused setups, not a compromise pick. Its 0.1g precision and USB-C charging are legitimate strengths. The limitation is platform size , it's compact enough that pour-over use with a wide-base vessel may feel constrained. For dedicated espresso use where USB-C charging is a priority, the [Maestri House Mini](product:maestri-house-mini-coffee-scale-with-timer) competes directly with Timemore variants rather than sitting below them.
### Do I need flow rate on a coffee scale, or is it just a marketing feature?

Flow rate measurement is a practical tool for both espresso and pour-over, not a spec-sheet decoration. For espresso, it shows extraction behavior in real time , a 1.5g/s flow looks and reads differently from a 2.5g/s flow, and both are diagnostic. For pour-over, it reveals whether your pour technique is consistent across intervals. If you're serious enough about brewing to buy a dedicated coffee scale, flow rate gives you information a basic timer alone cannot.
### Can I use a coffee scale for regular kitchen baking?
Yes, with caveats. The 2kg maximum capacity on these scales is limiting for large batch baking , flour for a big loaf can push past that ceiling quickly. The 0.1g resolution is more precision than baking requires. Any of the Timemore variants with ounce/kg unit switching will handle baking tasks comfortably within the capacity limit.
### How often does a coffee scale need to be recalibrated?

Recalibration frequency depends on how the scale is stored and used. Precision scales at 0.1g resolution can drift if stored incorrectly, exposed to sustained heat, or dropped. Most home users find recalibration necessary only after physical impact or when readings become obviously inconsistent , typically not a regular maintenance task. Using a calibration weight periodically to verify accuracy is good practice, particularly for espresso dosing where a 0.5g drift matters more than it would in any other brewing context.
