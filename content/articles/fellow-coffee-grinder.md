Fellow makes some of the most recognizable grinders on the market, and for good reason , the Ode line in particular has found a real home on specialty coffee countertops. But "Fellow grinder" covers a range of choices, and the right one depends on your brew method, your workflow, and how much you care about retention. I've been grinding specialty coffee long enough to have strong opinions about what actually matters here, and this is a straightforward assessment of the best options available. If you want context on how these fit into the broader landscape of [coffee grinders](/grinders/), that's worth reading before committing.

The short version: most buyers should be looking at the Ode Gen 2. But there are reasons to consider the alternatives, and a few reasons to look outside the Fellow ecosystem entirely.

## What to Look For in a Fellow Coffee Grinder

### Burr Type and Grind Consistency

The difference between a flat burr and a conical burr isn't marketing. Flat burrs , like the 64mm set in the Ode Gen 2 , tend to produce a more uniform particle size distribution, which matters most for filter methods where even extraction depends on consistent surface area. Conical burrs run cooler and quieter, and many home brewers find them perfectly adequate.

What both have over any blade grinder is categorical: burrs cut, blades smash. A blade grinder produces a mix of powder and chunks regardless of how long you run it. I ran a blade grinder for two weeks before understanding why my espresso was undrinkable , the uneven extraction wasn't a technique problem, it was a physics problem. Even a modest burr grinder is a genuine improvement, not a marginal one.

### Retention and Single-Dose Design

Retention is how much ground coffee stays inside the grinder after a dose. In a high-retention grinder, stale grounds from the previous session mix with fresh grounds in the next. If you're buying specialty coffee at serious prices per bag, you're throwing some of it away with every grind change.

Single-dose grinders , which hold only what you put in , solve this directly. The Ode was designed around this workflow from the start. For anyone grinding a different coffee most mornings, low retention isn't a luxury, it's the point.

### Grind Settings and Range

The number of grind settings matters less than the range they cover and how meaningfully they're spaced. Thirty-one settings sounds precise until you discover that half of them cluster in a range you'll never use.

For filter coffee , pour over, French press, cold brew , you need a grinder that handles medium-to-coarse well and offers enough adjustability to dial in extraction time. For espresso, you need a fundamentally different grind range, finer and more tightly spaced. The Ode Gen 2 is honest about this: it's a filter grinder. It does not pretend to be an espresso grinder, which is the right design call.

### Footprint and Workflow Integration

Counter space in a real kitchen is finite. A grinder that occupies the same footprint as a toaster but integrates cleanly into your morning workflow is worth more than a technically superior grinder you route around because it's awkward to use.

Look at where the grounds land, how the hopper loads, how the catch cup attaches, and whether the controls are accessible without looking at them. These small ergonomic details compound over years of daily use. The full range of [electric coffee grinders](/grinders/) varies considerably in how well manufacturers think through the actual use case versus the spec sheet.

### Noise and Build Quality

Electric burr grinders aren't silent, but there's a wide range. Motor quality, burr housing, and vibration damping all contribute. A grinder that rattles across the counter or requires both hands to hold steady during operation isn't a minor inconvenience , it's a daily tax.

Build quality is harder to assess from a listing. Look at what the housing is made from, whether the adjustment mechanism feels precise or vague, and whether the manufacturer has a track record of durable products. Fellow and OXO have established reputations in this space; lesser-known brands are a different calculation.

The [Fellow Ode Gen 2](product:fellow-ode-gen-2-64mm) is the clearest recommendation I can make for anyone serious about filter coffee at home. The 64mm flat burr set produces a consistent, unimodal particle distribution that you can taste in the cup , cleaner extraction, better clarity, more of what you paid for in your beans.

## Buying Guide

### Filter Coffee vs. Espresso

This is the first question that determines everything else. Filter grinders and espresso grinders operate at opposite ends of the grind range, and a grinder optimized for one rarely excels at the other.

The Ode Gen 2 is unambiguously a filter grinder. It handles pour over, French press, drip, and cold brew well. It does not handle espresso well, and Fellow is transparent about this. If your primary method is espresso , or if you plan to add an espresso machine within a year , the Ode is the wrong answer regardless of how good it is at what it does.

### Retention: Why It Matters More Than Most Reviews Acknowledge

High-retention grinders mix stale grounds from previous sessions into fresh grinds. For someone using one coffee, grinding the same dose daily, this matters less. For anyone rotating between bags or adjusting grind size between sessions, it matters considerably.

The Ode's single-dose design produces near-zero retention. You put in what you need, grind, and the chamber is empty. This is not just a workflow preference , it's a quality argument. The full category of [burr coffee grinders](/grinders/) ranges from low-retention single-dose designs to large-hopper models that hold enough beans for a week. Know which workflow you're actually buying for.

### Grind Setting Range and Adjustability

Thirty-one settings is a reasonable number for a filter grinder. What matters is how those settings are distributed and whether the adjustment mechanism gives you reliable repeatability.

Stepped adjusters , where each click is a distinct position , are more repeatable than stepless dials. If you're grinding the same recipe daily, stepped settings mean you can return to your preferred position without measuring. Vague or wobbly adjustment rings undermine this entirely. Test the mechanism feel if you can before buying; read for it in detailed reviews if you can't.

### Footprint and Counter Integration

Most home kitchen counters are shallower than manufacturers assume. Measure your available depth before ordering. A grinder that overhangs the counter edge or crowds the espresso machine is a daily friction point.

Beyond dimensions, consider the full workflow: where do you store the beans, where does the catch cup sit, where do the grounds land if the cup isn't perfectly positioned. Small design decisions , like whether the Ode's magnetic catch cup aligns cleanly , compound into either a seamless routine or a minor daily irritant.

### Brand and Long-Term Support

Fellow and OXO carry reputations built over years of products. Fellow in particular has invested in the specialty coffee market and built a customer base that talks loudly when something fails , which means problems surface quickly and the company has incentive to address them.

Lesser-known brands may offer competitive features at lower prices, but post-purchase support, warranty handling, and parts availability are harder to predict. For a grinder used daily over multiple years, this isn't a minor consideration. The premium for an established brand often buys more than the badge.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Fellow Ode Gen 2 suitable for espresso?

No , and Fellow is upfront about this. The Ode Gen 2 is designed for filter brewing: pour over, French press, drip, and cold brew. Its grind range doesn't extend fine enough for espresso, and the burr geometry is optimized for filter-range particle sizes. If espresso is your primary method, you need a different grinder entirely , one with a finer grind range and tighter step spacing at the low end.
### What is the difference between flat burr and conical burr grinders?

Flat burrs, like those in the [Fellow Ode Gen 2](product:fellow-ode-gen-2-64mm), tend to produce a more uniform particle size distribution , useful for filter methods where consistency drives extraction quality. Conical burrs run cooler and quieter, and typically retain fewer fines. For most home filter brewers, both produce excellent results; the gap matters more at higher precision levels than casual daily brewing demands.
### Should I buy the Fellow Ode Gen 2 or the OXO Brew Conical Burr Grinder?

If grind precision for filter coffee is the priority, the [Fellow Ode Gen 2](product:fellow-ode-gen-2-64mm) is the stronger technical choice , better burrs, single-dose workflow, and a design built specifically for that use case. The [OXO Brew Conical Burr Grinder](product:oxo-brew-conical-burr-grinder) offers more approachable operation and a trusted brand, and for brewers who value simplicity over maximum precision, it's a reasonable alternative. Choose Fellow if you want to optimize; choose OXO if you want reliable convenience.
### What does "single dose" mean and why does it matter?

Single dose means you load only the beans you plan to grind for a single brew session , no hopper, no stored beans. It matters because beans oxidize once exposed to air, and stored beans in a large hopper go stale faster than beans sealed in a bag. Single-dose grinders also allow you to switch between different coffees without mixing or purging, which is the right workflow if you're rotating between specialty bags.
### Is the Viesimple Gen 4 a reliable alternative to Fellow grinders?

The [Viesimple Gen 4](product:coffee-grinder-viesimple-gen-4) offers genuine features , single-dose design, magnetic dosing cup, anti-static construction , at a lower entry point than the Fellow lineup. The uncertainty is long-term reliability. Viesimple doesn't have the same established track record as Fellow or OXO, so while the out-of-box experience may be competitive, durability over years of daily use is less predictable. It's a reasonable consideration for budget-constrained buyers who understand that tradeoff.
